Why paying the highest interest loans first (the ‘avalanche’ strategy) doesn’t always win

Federal student loans charge simple interest. Interest you haven’t paid yet builds up as an interest balance: a wall in front of the principal. The wall itself charges nothing, but every payment has to cut through it before it can touch the principal.

So an extra dollar only starts saving you interest once you’re through a loan’s wall. Avalanche ranks loans by rate alone, so it can pour money into a wall while a slightly cheaper loan with no wall keeps charging interest.

The best order depends on how big each wall is, how fast your minimum payments wear it down, and how long each loan has left. The calculator searches month by month for the plan that pays the least. Avalanche is one of the plans it compares, so it never does worse than avalanche.
